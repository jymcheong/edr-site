Threat briefing · Special report

The soft target myth: how non-profits became prime adversary targets.

Non-profits don't have corporate balance sheets, but they hold goldmines: refugee records, dissident communications, donor financial networks and critical frontline infrastructure. Adversaries know this.

Real incidents: the cost of under-protection

  • 515,000+
    records breached

    International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC)

    A sophisticated, state-aligned intrusion compromised the personal data and confidential locations of over 515,000 vulnerable people fleeing war, disaster and persecution. The "Restoring Family Links" humanitarian pipeline was halted worldwide.

  • 136
    countries paralysed

    SOS Children's Villages global outage

    Ransomware crippled child protection services and emergency relief across 136 territories. Aid workers were cut off from communication channels, food stipend disbursements and beneficiary safety registries for days.

  • 0-click
    spyware targeting

    Human rights defenders and civil society

    Amnesty International and Citizen Lab investigations repeatedly uncover advanced commercial spyware implants weaponised against regional NGO directors, legal aid defenders and whistleblowers to intercept field operations.

Why adversaries target the sector

  • Irreplaceable human data.

    Compromising an enterprise exposes credit cards, which are replaceable. Compromising an NGO exposes political refugees, abuse victims or undercover humanitarian workers, which is life-critical.

  • The vendor pricing trap.

    Enterprise security vendors require 100+ minimum seat licences ($25k–$70k a year). NGOs run on tight grants with strict "overhead" caps, which forces them onto consumer antivirus.

  • High organisational fluidity.

    Volunteers, field laptops, temporary staff and decentralised partner networks create massive, porous attack surfaces.

The edr.sg mandate

Defense cannot remain an exclusive luxury of Fortune 500 enterprises. We build automated, zero-overhead deception and vaccination tools so resource-constrained defenders can stop automated campaigns without enterprise retainers.
